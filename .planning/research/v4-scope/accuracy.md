## Recommendation

Make the next milestone **Measured NTSC accuracy**, beginning with the small tune-up already required by SEED-006, then a complete conformance baseline and a bounded set of fixes chosen from that baseline. Do not promise every AccuracyCoin test or combine regional timing, expansion audio and FDS into that milestone. PAL/Dendy should be the next substantial compatibility milestone if the baseline does not reveal a fundamental NTSC timing defect. A narrowly selected simple mapper can follow, or move ahead of regions if a specific host/player has a concrete game requirement and a licence-safe fixture.

The strongest alternative is PAL-first: players gain a visible new library immediately, whereas some extreme NTSC quirks have little gameplay effect. Accuracy-first wins provisionally because the product's central promise is console behaviour and the present evidence cannot quantify its largest accuracy gaps. A two-stage measurement and prioritization checkpoint avoids spending months chasing exotic score points. If a complete baseline shows only revision-sensitive corner cases remaining and the existing playable fixtures stay sound, move directly to regions.

## Live AccuracyCoin change and validation implications

Keep the old-pin result alongside the upgrade report, then gate keys only within each identical pin. Report pass, fail, skip, not-run and timeout separately; DRAW entries must not increase the denominator. Run-all can stop at a hanging test, so bounded per-page or individual-test continuation is valuable diagnostic work. Prefer an owned small parser and controller driver over a dependency, ROM modification, or emulator-specific hook. Mutation-check missing results, duplicate names, malformed pointers, controller held too long, fake completion and a hung test. Determine frame caps by observation rather than retaining 1800 for every suite.

The target is NTSC RP2A03G/RP2C02G; passing codes allow hardware variants. Never convert accepted variant codes into failures to match one emulator. A licensed test ROM is primary evidence for its asserted behaviour, not proof every assertion is settled hardware truth. Corroborate contested timing with circuit documentation or hardware reports; use released oracle binaries only. Scores from different commits are incomparable. The old seed's 136-point accuracy-class shorthand is not a portable acceptance threshold on the new denominator.

Regional clock/divider and scanline differences are documented in [NESdev cycle chart](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/Cycle_reference_chart) (T2; live search available, direct fetch returned 403). In particular PAL CPU/PPU is 16/5, Dendy 15/5; Dendy's delayed vblank and PAL's distinct APU/DMA behaviour prevent treating either as slowed NTSC. Preparation section 8 and CPU/APU section 6 retain revision-pinned detail pending phase research.

Mapper selection should use validation readiness and actual demand, not the stale dump ranking. [Holy Mapperel](https://github.com/pinobatch/holy-mapperel) (T1) provides permissive fixtures for 9, 10, 66 and 69, but explicitly tests gross IRQ/board behaviour rather than exact timing. Its supported-board documentation gives realistic examples: Punch-Out!! for 9; Famicom Wars/Fire Emblem for 10; Gimmick! for 69. Commercial examples describe value only; no ROMs are requested or acquired. [Mapper 206](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/INES_Mapper_206), [MMC5](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/MMC5), [MMC5 audio](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/MMC5_audio) (T2) show why 206 and MMC5 belong in different complexity classes. MMC5's scanline detector is not MMC3 A12 IRQ with a new number.

## Proposed milestone boundaries and exit evidence

1. **Tune-up plus evidence:** close the separately researched small v3 debt; inventory old and new pins, implement full run/diagnostics, and publish measured counts including unresolved results. Pin, licence, SHA, decoder, scoreboard and fixture hashes move in one reviewed change. Existing 17 protected results remain accounted for.
2. **Choose and repair one bounded cluster:** prioritize DMA/interrupt/controller failures affecting games, then PPU bus/sprite failures. Each fix gets a smallest owned reproducer and the external failing test, state continuation/reject-load coverage if fields change, and a documented hardware source. Decide the number/names of mandatory fixes after the baseline; an all-pass objective requires a separately justified estimate.
3. **Cross-deliverable closeout:** full conformance observation, six-platform deterministic hashes, libretro/runner parity on gameplay fixtures, `cmake --workflow --preset ci`, and benchmark/Cachegrind comparison. Accuracy fixes can intentionally move hashes; preserve old evidence and explain the new hardware-correct output. Use the existing reviewed performance exception mechanism if a necessary correction exceeds +1%, not hidden scheduling shortcuts.

Explicit exclusions: PAL/Dendy implementation, new mapper families, expansion audio, FDS, extra controllers, rendering filters, BIOS/database bundles, dependency adoption and leaderboard submission. Host-facing completeness belongs in this milestone only where required to expose truthful region/support information or fix an existing contract; unsolicited cheats, achievements and controller menus do not improve NTSC correctness.

For playable proof, extend the already licensed `runner.nes`/existing fixtures first (`tests/roms/manifest.txt:23`, `tests/CMakeLists.txt:1130`). The boot-to-play test already includes negative controls and mutants; add a distinct state restoration or interaction outcome rather than another screenshot. The earlier DABG pin changed from GPL to a verified zlib revision (`.planning/preparation/CONFORMANCE.md`, pin correction): always inspect the exact selected revision's code **and asset** licences. New mapper support needs both targeted owned tests and a permitted public fixture; commercial ROM possession is not a CI strategy.

## Bounded unknowns that change the roadmap

- Full baseline: one fixed-pin run with capped fallback pages, retain every result byte and runtime; identify hangs/prerequisite failures. This determines whether NTSC fixes are one milestone or several.
- Cost: measure full suite wall time on the existing CI runner before choosing PR versus nightly partitions. Keep essential probes on PRs and total inventory nightly if too costly.
- Hardware disputes: list exact failing variants and source contradictions; a bounded oracle-binary experiment can locate divergence but cannot settle hardware alone.
- PAL evidence: locate permissively licensed region-aware fixtures or estimate a few owned timing ROMs before committing a region milestone. Confirm audio buffers and state bounds at 50 Hz.
- Demand: one named supported host workflow or player title can promote a simple mapper or peripheral. No concrete demand currently justifies FDS ahead of accuracy and regions.

Overall recommendation confidence: MEDIUM. The measurement gap and 146-test upstream change are directly verified; effort and player impact remain estimates until the bounded baseline exists.

## Adversarial pass

I read the reliability and governance reports. **Recommend the proposed three-phase v4 “NTSC conformance and execution safety,” with a discovery gate before Phase C and no unnamed hardware-fix commitment.** The common outcome is credible evidence about the already supported machine: trustworthy host assertions, complete conformance observation, and execution coverage beyond parsing. Governance is a small boundary procedure, not a fourth product objective. A baseline-only milestone would be coherent, but the owned execution harness closes a distinct reachability gap cheaply enough to include provisionally.

**Strongest counterargument:** this combines three independently useful efforts under “trust,” yet delivers no newly playable hardware and can expand indefinitely when fuzzing exposes defects. Accuracy may require a large CPU/DMA redesign; lifecycle fuzz can independently expose mapper/state bugs; RetroArch retries can become a host protocol investigation. The existing state target already executes two frames after accepted mutations, so labeling all execution untested would inflate the case. A small maintainer can get more player value by measuring the baseline, fixing one game-relevant defect, then delivering PAL. This objection wins if the discovery gate shows that the new target mostly duplicates state-fuzz reach or needs major runtime restructuring.

**Keep the three phases only with these boundaries:** A selects precisely WR-01/02/03 and truthful MMC3 metadata plus the ledger procedure; no general audit cleanup, broad admission corrections or cross-platform RetroArch expansion. Negative sequence tests must fail; lost idempotent requests may retry, while FRAMEADVANCE/PAUSE_TOGGLE need explicit confirmation rather than blind replay. B migrates the pin/name decoder, inventories every test, and produces complete bounded observations. Its exit is an honest inventory, not a passing score. Record 146 scored tests and five DRAW entries separately; every scored entry has pass/fail/skip/not-run/timeout evidence, with prerequisite failures and hangs visible. Retain the 17 existing protected passes and old-pin comparison; count/key changes are reviewable.

C begins with one short instrumented prototype and a written campaign contract: all six accepted board families, bounded bank sizes/frame budgets, fixed lifecycle operations, controlled input, sanitizer/resource limits and deterministic reproduction. Compare its reachable paths with loader and state targets; use measurements to fix PR replay and nightly budgets before promotion. Its deliverable is one owned target plus replay corpus and documented halt/progress properties. No new mapper/region, fuzz engine or generic framework; do not make “fix all fuzz bugs” a requirement. Repair discovered supported-profile safety defects only within the approved implementation boundary. If a confirmed defect requires architectural expansion, stop completion and present the minimal fix/rejection option and measured scope change; neither silently park a critical defect nor claim universal safety from a finite campaign. Ordinary infinite NES loops and JAM are explicitly classified outcomes.

SEED-003 is **partial**: conformance measurement advances it, while all-pass accuracy and PAL/Dendy remain unfulfilled. SEED-004 is **partial**: performance gates/benchmarks and state fuzz already shipped; accepted-program execution extends its remaining robustness half. SEED-006 is **recurring**, never globally completed by one tune-up. After B, material gameplay/timing gaps make a named NTSC fix milestone preferred Near. Otherwise promote PAL, followed by independently justified Dendy; both require regional fixtures, audio/state/AV contracts and separate acceptance. Simple mapper demand remains an alternative, not technically blocked by every NTSC corner case.
